# 4. Open access to research infrastructures as a pillar of open science

Open access for advanced scientific technologies and the expertise needed to use them supports Open Science by promoting:

-   more efficient use of public funding,
-   greater research effectiveness,
-   fairer access to high-end resources for organisations with limited research budgets.

Research infrastructures (RIs) play a critical role in this by offering services and support that enable scientific progress. Their goals include:

-   providing access to instrumentation, expertise, actual data, and scientific services,
-   supporting research projects, training, communication, and policy-related activities.

Access to RIs can take various forms:

-   physical - in person accessing specialised instrumentations or labs
-   remote - e.g. access resources and services *without physically visiting* the infrastructure/facility (e.g. remote operation of high-performance computing centers or submitting jobs/workflows remotely).
-   virtual - free and open access via communication networks to digital tools and data, where many users can utilize the service simultaneously without selection

European countries and the European Commission have established a legal framework for pan-European Research Infrastructures, called ERIC = European Research Infrastructure Consortium, with an aim to ensure sharing of and open access to infrastructures and have a forum for it at ESFRI. It is a legal form like GmbH or Ltd or other forms used by companies which provides legal personalities to consortia of research infrastructures with shared technology or science field, and enables them to operate.
