Following this course, participants will be able to:

1.  **Distinguish** between EIROforum and ESFRI/ERICs as structurally different but complementary coordination models, and explain what each offers to researchers and infrastructure professionals working at a facility level.
2.  **Explain** how ERICs use User Access Programs to coordinate access across distributed member facilities, identifying the operational elements — eligibility, review, scheduling, costing, and user communication — that make such programs work in practice.
3.  **Compare** the operational models of at least two ERICs (e.g. Instruct-ERIC and ELIXIR), analysing how differences in service type, funding structure, and governance lead to different access conditions for researchers.
4.  **Design** the core structure of a User Access Program for a research platform, drawing on ERIC examples, and justify the choices made around access modes, eligibility criteria, review process, and user communication.

## Competencies for research infrastructure scientists

This course helps develop the following competencies for research infrastructure scientists.

**Knowledge**

Plan, design and operate a sustainable service:

- Understands standards and best practices for service delivery

Identify and support users' needs:

- Understands users activities
- Knows about customer support practices
- Recognizes sensitivity of customer-supplier relationships

Build relationships and collaborations:

- Knows the types of stakeholders (user communities, policymakers, Board of Governors, etc.), their interests and their sphere of influence
- Understands strategies and techniques for engagement and professional relationship-building

**Skills**

Plan, design and operate a sustainable service:

- Delivers effective services to distributed user base
- Raises awareness of services to the user community
- Sees the bigger picture of how own service fits into the organisational and wider landscape

Build relationships and collaborations:

- Uses networks effectively and actively to facilitate work, gain support, and achieve shared goals.

**Attitudes**

Identify and support user needs:

- Maintains a user- and service-oriented mindset
- Seeks opportunities to engage with users to understand their needs, goals, strategies, frustrations and feedback.

Build relationships and collaborations:

- Recognises the importance of building and maintaining internal and external relationships
- Actively seeks collaborative partners, shares networks and encourages this behaviour in others
- Appreciates the importance of compromise and avoids overcommitting the organisation or providing an unrealistic view of how it can benefit stakeholders

Monitor innovation and technology trends:

- Demonstrates curiosity and open mindset for developments outside the immediate field and how they might apply to own service ('thinking outside of the box')

Know about legal framework:

- Communicates early and in a transparent manner with all internal stakeholders, and ensures necessary follow-up

Communicate and broadcast information clearly:

- Proactively seeks to discover different audiences and their interests
