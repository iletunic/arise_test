# Part 3: Latest developments

## What’s on the Horizon for EOSC?

EOSC is entering a critical political and strategic phase as the EU prepares the next **Multiannual Financial Framework (MFF)**, the EU’s next seven-year budget, which determines how funding is allocated across major policy areas, including research and innovation, over several years. One of the main vehicles for EU research is currently the Horizon Europe programme; its successor (FP10) is expected to continue from 2028 onwards.

Current discussions suggest that the European Commission may reduce the number of European Partnerships in FP10, which creates uncertainty about the long-term structure and funding model of EOSC. In response, the EOSC Association and many research organisations argue strongly that EOSC should continue, because it is no longer seen as an Open Science initiative “only”, but increasingly as strategic infrastructure for Europe’s digital and data sovereignty, AI competitiveness and cross-disciplinary research capacity.

The Commission, the Member States and the Parliament are currently negotiating the research budget of the EU for the period 2028–2034. Horizon Europe will remain, but in a different shape and form, and this is to be determined over roughly the next twelve months. It is therefore not clear how EOSC will develop further.

There are clear indications that research data are valued as a strategic asset in Europe and that, particularly in the context of AI, data must continue to be shared, reused and verified. At the same time, the Commission wants to simplify its programmes. After 2028, EOSC could remain as it is, or it could evolve into a larger programme that also includes computing power, that is, a bigger umbrella partnership that also covers artificial intelligence.

Until then, a last work programme for Horizon Europe will still include calls of the INFRAEOSC type and EOSC projects such as OSCARS. Funding opportunities for anyone interested will therefore continue for at least a few more years, regardless of what is decided in the next EU budget, which starts in 2028.

## Closing remarks

EOSC is a moving target. Learners should keep following how the field develops and decide whether it is of interest to them. Those who wish to take part in initiatives and discussions about standards and similar topics have, as shown in [Part 2](chapter_02.md), ways of doing so, although this is optional.

Learners should also watch for funding opportunities. After the ARISE programme, such opportunities could provide the means for funding the next steps or small projects of their own.
