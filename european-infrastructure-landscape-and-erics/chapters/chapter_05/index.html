5. Exercises and quiz

5.1 Suggestions for exercises

Exercise 1: What are research infrastructures for you? Do you already know?

Instruction: interactive poll, warming up

Duration: 3 minutes

Timing: right at the beginning

Poll questions:

  • “What are research infrastructures for you?”
  • “Have you heard of ERICs/Research Infrastructures before?”
  • “Do you know whether your country/organisation is a member of any ERIC?”
  • “Which domain do you think has the most ERICs?”

Purpose: Collect baseline knowledge and personalise the lecture.

Exercise 2: Discussion - how would you give all European researchers access to the infrastructure they need?

Instructions and format: Divide in small groups (3–4 people) to discuss what solutions you would propose to solve this challenge. Each group to the presenter in advance. This exercise works well either at the begining of the course or at the end. Alternatively, do the exercise at the begining and re-visit the answers at the end of the course.

Duration: 6 min discussion in groups + 5 mins presentations of their solutions.

Examples of possible outcomes
  • A network of infrastructures across Europe, with EU money to strengthen existing facilities and create new ones where needed.
  • A single portal or entry point, organised by discipline, showing who offers which service and how to reach them; a catalogue and cloud services to annotate what is available.
  • Training and more communication between facility staff; staff exchange.
  • Centralised or national, government-run facilities to which samples or people can go; hubs of specialisation with shared machines bookable from all over Europe; shared software resources.
  • Funding for researcher mobility, and flexible budgets that are not tied to single nations.
  • Financial incentives for facilities to join, and trained staff in each facility to coordinate external users, whose projects often do not fit the equipment exactly.
  • Funding for users, since most facilities charge for services.
  • Standards for data and software sharing.

Exercise 3: Fix this broken access process (diagnosis case)

Instructions: divide trainees in groups of 3 and identified what went wrong in the following scenario (Which steps of a user access program were missing?):

Duration: 10 mins

Example:

  • A user submits a proposal.
  • No technical check is done.
  • A reviewer finds out too late that the facility lacks capacity.
  • The user is rejected after 8 weeks.
Possible answers
  • No moderator oversight
  • Missing technical evaluation
  • No timeline management
  • No communication rules
  • No quality assurance mechanism

Exercise 4: Plan the access

Instructions: get the trainees to chose a service they would use, and ask them to provide a bullet point application. Provide a list of services that is relevant to the background of the trainees, and ask them to chose from that list. Ask the trainees to keep in mind how they would mitigate the risks which could lead to the unwanted application failure (feasibility, poor communication, complexity of experiments/analysis etc).

Duration: 5 mins presentation of the exercise, 10 mins access application, 5 mins discussion

Exercise 5: Design the evaluation criteria (5 min)

Instructions:Present the list of criteria below and ask participants to vote if the following criteria are relevant or should be relevant for the selection of users that are requesting access to the service which the participants are providing. Use mentimeter for voting. Each person votes on their own. Ask to share the thoughts they had when considering these criteria.

Criteria examples:

  • Scientific excellence
  • Technical feasibility
  • Feasibility within time window
  • Budget availability
  • User experience with technology
  • Safety risks
  • Novelty
  • Country membership
  • Training/learning objective
  • Potential impact on the community

Exercise 6: What if … discussions

Instructions: raise following questions to the group and ask them to provide suggestions on how to solve the following situations (see below). Participants are grouped in the groups of 3-4 persons, for 7 minutes breakout. Groups chose two or three situations and report back, and the trainer comments.

Duration: 15 mins

Situations to discuss:

  • User’s project is excellent but technically impossible?
  • The facility is fully booked for 4 months. What to tell the user?
  • User fails to acknowledge the facility in their publication after the access?
  • The proposal requires a biosafety level that the chosen centre does not have.
  • Virtual access/open services require technical support?
Examples of possible outcomes
Situation Suggestions from the groups Comment from the Instruct speaker
Excellent but technically impossible Educate users beforehand about what the equipment can do; help adjust the proposal; set up a development project (funding is the difficulty); split the project among facilities; redirect through a coordinator; ask other facilities or experts Training starts at proposal stage: the user is told what to do first
Fully booked for four months Redirect to another facility with the same equipment; offer a slot and prepare so all is ready; waiting list; ask whether the user can wait; shorter booking slots; priority rules; night shifts Instruct suggests other facilities; some services run 24/7, some do not. A central coordinator is needed, since a facility cannot know who else can help. Demand data can be reported to funders to argue for more capacity. Funding is locked to a proposal for six months.
No acknowledgement in publication Clear instructions up front; signed agreement beforehand; contact first and corresponding authors, then the journal; ask for an erratum; contact users before publication or ask for a preprint; journals could require a statement of which facility gave access; blacklist only as an extreme measure after repeat offences Hard to detect, especially with common surnames. Instruct sends an automatic message six months after the visit. Discussed but not done: counting unacknowledged publications against later applications.
Biosafety level not available Redirect to another centre in the network; if demand keeps rising, build capacity at that level Preparedness matters: report rising need to funders. Instruct can connect users to other life science infrastructures that handle higher biosafety levels.
Virtual access needs technical support Dedicated, funded technical support staff; support after delivery where possible; training or support for first use (no separate comment)

5.2 Quiz for self-assessment

The questions below are an example of a multiple-choice quiz that can be used for (self-)assessment after this course.

Purpose: Check understanding of the concepts

Type: Multiple choice quiz

Format: Online (Mentimeter, Slido, google forms…)

The correct answers are listed at the end of the quiz.

Example questions:

  1. What does ERIC stand for?
    • A) European Research Innovation Council
    • B) European Research Infrastructure Consortium
    • C) European Research Integration Committee
    • D) European Research Investment Council
  2. Which of the following is a key role of an ERIC?
    • A) Provide legal advice to countries
    • B) Facilitate access to research infrastructure
    • C) Coordinate all access to RIs in the country
    • D) Conduct independent research
  3. Which of the following steps is part of a User Access Program?
    • A) Proposal submission
    • B) Building the infrastructure
    • C) Hiring new staff
    • D) Selling technology
  4. ERICs can be made up of:
    • A) Only countries
    • B) Only universities
    • C) Countries and international organizations
    • D) Individual labs
  5. What is the main benefit of distributed ERIC infrastructures?
    • A) Reduce research costs and share expertise
    • B) Centralize all research in one country
    • C) Broadens access to a cutting edge services
    • D) Avoid international collaboration
  6. What is the main benefit of a structured User Access Program in an ERIC?
    • A) It ensures fair, transparent, and efficient access to facilities
    • B) It increases the attractiveness of the services
    • C) It reduces the need for facility staff to interact with users

Time: 3–5 minutes

Answers

Q1: B; Q2: B; Q3: A; Q4: C; Q5: A; Q6: A
