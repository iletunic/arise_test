Research Infrastructure Scientists training materials: how to use and contribute

Open training materials for the ARISE 2 programme. Each course has its own description page, chapters with theoretical knowledge, examples and exercises, and references.

What is this?

These materials train future and current research infrastructure experts. They are made in the scope of the ARISE program at EMBL (www.embl.org/arise) for future leading positions in life science research infrastructures (RIs): core facilities, data service teams, synchrotrons and other service platforms. They were written following multiple editions of the courses, each improving on the previous one, and are a product of teamwork by several people per topic (5–8).

This teamwork was necessary to combine community-owned elements: theory, best-practice examples and relevant exercises.

Who is it for?

  • Trainers who want to run a course on one of these topics: use the course description, timing, examples and exercises.
  • Researchers and RI staff who want to read about a topic or refresh their memory.

You are free to reuse the materials, with credit (see Licence below).

How to find your way around

Every course has the same structure: - Course description: learning objectives, competencies, key concepts, timing and credits. - Chapters: the knowledge material. Boxes labelled Example show how things are done in practice. Boxes labelled Exercise are for trainers to use in a session, and suggested answers are hidden until you open them. - References: sources and further reading.

Call to contribute

  • The material comes from the direct experience of a few trainers, with their own field, type of user and type of access. Your experience makes it useful for more people. We would like colleagues across Europe to use it to prepare training for their local RI community, and a diversity of examples helps with that.
  • Theory about research infrastructures is not yet gathered in one reference book. We want to collect community knowledge here.
  • We add your name to the authors of the course for recognition and visibility. If you prefer not to be credited, just tell us.

How to contribute

Choose the way that suits you. You do not need to know Git. 1. Send us an email. Write what you would like to change or add, and on which course and page. Contact: see the end of this file. 2. Open an issue on GitHub at https://github.com/ricapacity/RI_training. Name the course and the page, and describe your suggestion. 3. Edit the page yourself. Click the pencil icon (“Edit this page”) at the top of the page you want to change. GitHub (free account needed) turns your change into a suggestion, which the editors review. If you do not see the icon, use option 2.

What we would especially like

  • Corrections of facts, links or wording.
  • New examples from your own institute or infrastructure. Please say: the technology field, the institute, the country, the type of access and the type of user.
  • New exercises that trainers can use.
  • Updates where numbers, services or policies have changed. Some topics move quickly, for example EOSC (the size of the EOSC Association, the nodes in the Federation, open calls and the EU budget discussions).

Writing tips

  • Short paragraphs, plain English, one idea at a time.
  • Give a source or link for facts and numbers.
  • Only add images you own or that are licensed for reuse, and say where they come from. Do not add photos of people without their permission.

What happens next?

The editors read your suggestion, may ask you a question, and then add it. We will credit you as an author.
